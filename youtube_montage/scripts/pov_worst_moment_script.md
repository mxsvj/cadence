# POV: You Invest €10,000 at the Worst Possible Moment — voice-over script

Format « boîtes », 6 chapitres, style sombre avec une couleur par chapitre, voix Emma (Kokoro). Durée : 5:31.
Chaque ligne = un plan. Les chiffres sont vérifiés (sources en bas) ; les formulations prudentes (« about », « often », « usually ») sont voulues.

## Intro

- POV: it's October 2007. You've saved €10,000, and you finally put it all into the stock market.
- You don't know it yet, but you just bought at the very top.
- This is a story about the worst timing imaginable, and what happened next.
- The numbers are real S&P 500 data, rounded, in dollar terms. Past returns don't predict future ones. This is not financial advice.
- Six parts. Let's go back to 2007.

## 01 — The record

- Part 1. The record.
- On October 9th, 2007, the S&P 500 closes at a record: 1,565 points.
- Everyone is optimistic. House prices have been rising for years. Banks are lending easily.
- You've read that the stock market goes up in the long run. You're 30, and you won't need this money for years.
- So you invest your €10,000, and you feel smart.

## 02 — The fall

- Part 2. The fall.
- Then American homeowners stop paying their mortgages. Those loans had been packaged and sold to banks all over the world, including in Europe.
- On September 15th, 2008, Lehman Brothers, a giant American bank, goes bankrupt.
- A few weeks later, Iceland's banks collapse. Governments across Europe rush to rescue their own.
- In Belgium and the Netherlands, the bank Fortis has to be rescued by three governments.
- Markets fall almost every week. Your €10,000 becomes €8,000. Then €6,000.
- Your colleagues stop talking about shares. At a dinner, someone says the stock market is just a casino.

## 03 — The bottom

- Part 3. The bottom.
- On March 9th, 2009, the S&P 500 closes at 677. It has lost 57% since the day you bought.
- Your €10,000 is now worth about €4,300, on paper.
- You check your account online once. Then you decide to stop looking.
- Unemployment is rising. You start wondering if your own job is safe.
- Every instinct tells you to sell, before it goes to zero.
- And this is the moment that decides everything.

## 04 — The wait

- Part 4. The long wait.
- Imagine two versions of you. The first one sells at the bottom, to stop the pain. The second one does nothing.
- Version one feels relief. The pain stops. But the losses stay.
- The market starts climbing, but slowly. A year later, it's still far below the price you paid.
- In 2010, Greece needs a rescue. Then Ireland, and Portugal. Every year brings a new reason to give up.
- In 2011, the market drops again, by almost 20%.
- Version one is waiting for things to calm down before buying back. Things never feel calm.
- With dividends reinvested, version two is back to €10,000 in April 2012. About four and a half years after the peak.

## 05 — The new high

- Part 5. The new high.
- In 2012, the head of the European Central Bank says he will do whatever it takes to save the euro. Slowly, the fear fades.
- And on March 28th, 2013, even without dividends, the index finally beats its 2007 record.
- Version two still owns the same shares as before. Nothing was lost, except some sleep.
- Version one sold at about €4,300, and bought back much higher, or never came back at all.
- The worst timing didn't ruin version two. Selling ruined version one.

## 06 — Today

- Part 6. Fast forward.
- There were more drops on the way, in 2020 and in 2022. Each time, it felt like the end. Each time, so far, it wasn't.
- By the end of 2024, the S&P 500 was almost four times its 2007 record, before counting dividends.
- So the €10,000 invested at the worst possible moment grew to almost €38,000 in index terms, and more with dividends.
- For a European, currency moves change the exact number, up or down. But the lesson doesn't change.
- And if you had kept investing €200 a month through the crash, you would have bought shares at the lowest prices of the decade.
- Bad timing hurts. But time fixes most of it, if you let it.

## Outro

- Three things decided this story. Not panicking. Not needing the money. And waiting long enough.
- Not every market recovers this fast. Japan's took 34 years. That's why many investors spread their money across the world.
- Past returns don't predict future ones, and nothing here is a recommendation. This is not financial advice.
- Would you have sold? It's easy to say no, fifteen years later.
- If this story made you think, subscribe. Next up, what happens when you invest €200 a month for thirty years.
