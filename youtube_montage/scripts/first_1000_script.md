# How to Invest Your First €1,000 So Simply It Feels Illegal

Sources (vérifiées en octobre 2026) :

- Investor Compensation Schemes Directive 97/9/EC (at least €20,000 per investor) : https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1997/9/oj
- S&P 500 drawdown history (2020: −34%, record again in August 2020) : https://dqydj.com/sp-500-drawdown-history/

Hypothetical examples at 7% a year: €1,000 left alone for 30 years ≈ €7,600; with €100 a month added ≈ €130,000. No brand, broker or fund is recommended. Tax accounts and rules differ by country.

## Texte dit (= sous-titres)

0:00 You've saved your first €1,000. Now what?
0:04 Your bank offers a fund. A friend talks about crypto. Someone online has a secret strategy.
0:10 So you do nothing, and the money just sits there, losing a little to inflation every year.
0:16 And here's the part nobody tells you.
0:19 Your first €1,000 isn't about getting rich. It's about building the habit that will.
0:25 Here are the eight steps, in order. Let's begin.

### 1 · Kill expensive debt first

0:29 One. Kill expensive debt first.
0:31 If you have a credit card or a loan at 15 or 20% a year, pay that off first.
0:37 Every euro of that debt you repay earns you that rate, guaranteed. No investment promises that.

### 2 · Keep a small safety cushion

0:44 Two. Keep a small safety cushion.
0:46 Before investing, keep some cash for surprises: a broken phone, a car repair, a big bill.
0:52 Start with one month of expenses, and build towards three to six. Otherwise, the first emergency forces you to sell at the worst moment.

### 3 · Use the right account

1:01 Three. Use the right account.
1:04 Many European countries have accounts with tax advantages. The PEA in France. The ISA in the UK. The ISK in Sweden.
1:12 The same investment, inside the right account, can leave you with noticeably more after tax. Check what your country offers.

### 4 · Pick a regulated, low-cost broker

1:20 Four. Pick a regulated, low-cost broker.
1:23 Look for a broker regulated in the EU or the UK. In the EU, if a broker fails and can't give your investments back, compensation schemes cover at least €20,000.
1:34 Then look at the fees: per order, per year, and for currency exchange. On €1,000, a €10 fee is already 1%.

### 5 · Buy the whole market

1:43 Five. Buy the whole market.
1:45 Instead of guessing which company will win, buy them all, with one global index ETF.
1:51 One fund can hold well over a thousand companies across dozens of countries, for around 0.2% a year or less. In Europe, look for UCITS in the name.

### 6 · Automate it

2:02 Six. Automate it.
2:04 Many brokers let you set up a monthly savings plan, sometimes for free. Even €50 a month.
2:10 Your first €1,000 becomes the start of a habit, not a one-off bet.
2:16 €1,000 left alone for thirty years at 7% a year grows to about €7,600. Add €100 a month, and it's about €130,000.

### 7 · Then don't touch it

2:28 Seven. Then don't touch it.
2:31 In early 2020, stock markets fell about a third in a few weeks. People who sold locked in the loss.
2:37 The S&P 500 was back at its record about five months after the bottom.
2:42 Don't check it every day. Once a month is plenty. Once a year is better.

### 8 · Keep learning, slowly

2:48 Eight. Keep learning, slowly.
2:50 Read one good book. Watch the fees. Ignore anyone promising fast, guaranteed returns.
2:56 If it sounds too good to be true, it usually is.

### Final message

3:00 Final message.
3:02 The best first investment isn't a stock. It's a system: no expensive debt, a cushion, the right account, one cheap global fund, and automatic deposits.
3:12 Boring? Yes. That's exactly why it works.
