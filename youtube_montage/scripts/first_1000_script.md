# How to Invest Your First €1,000 (8 Steps, in Order)

Durée : 4:20 · style doodle · voix Kokoro bf_emma

Sources (vérifiées en octobre 2026) :

- Investor Compensation Schemes Directive 97/9/EC (at least €20,000 per investor) : https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1997/9/oj
- S&P 500 drawdown history (price index): 2020 bottom 23 March, record again 18 August 2020. After 2000: −49% to October 2002, back to the 2000 peak in May 2007, then −57% to March 2009, and above the 2000 peak for good from March 2013 : https://dqydj.com/sp-500-drawdown-history/
- UCITS Directive 2009/65/EC : https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/65/oj

Hypothetical examples at 7% a year (effective annual rate): €1,000 left alone for 30 years ≈ €7,600; with €100 a month added ≈ €125,000 in total, of which about €37,000 is money you put in. Before fees, inflation and tax. Investing can lose money. Pick a broker authorised by a financial regulator (in the EU, a broker authorised in another member state is fine). The EU investor compensation scheme covers at least €20,000 per investor when a firm fails and cannot return your assets (some countries pay 90% of the claim); it does not cover market losses. In a PEA, only PEA-eligible ETFs can be held (global trackers there are synthetic). No brand, broker or fund is recommended. Tax accounts and rules differ by country.

## Texte dit (= sous-titres)

0:00 Everyone has a plan for your first €1,000. Most of them are wrong.
0:05 Your bank offers a fund. A friend talks about crypto. Someone online has a secret strategy.
0:12 So you do nothing. And inflation eats a little of it every year.
0:17 But your first €1,000 isn't about getting rich.
0:20 It's about building the habit that will.
0:23 Eight steps, in order. One warning: you can lose money.

### 1 · Kill expensive debt first

0:28 One. Kill expensive debt first.
0:32 If you have a credit card or a loan at 15 or 20% a year, pay that off first.
0:37 Every euro you pay back is 20% you stop paying. No investment can promise that.

### 2 · Keep a small safety cushion

0:44 Two. Keep a small safety cushion.
0:47 Before investing, keep some cash for surprises: a broken phone, a car repair, a big bill.
0:53 Start with one month of expenses, and build towards three to six. Otherwise, the first emergency forces you to sell at the worst moment.
1:01 And if €1,000 is all you have? Then that's your cushion. Keep it, and start with a small monthly plan instead.

### 3 · Use the right account

1:10 Three. Use the right account.
1:13 Many European countries have accounts with tax advantages. The PEA in France. The ISA in the UK. The ISK in Sweden.
1:22 The same investment, inside the right account, can leave you with noticeably more after tax. Check what your country offers.

### 4 · Pick a regulated, low-cost broker

1:30 Four. Pick a regulated, low-cost broker.
1:35 Look for a broker authorised by a financial regulator. In the EU, if a broker fails and can't give your investments back, a compensation scheme steps in, usually up to €20,000 or more.
1:47 Then look at the fees: per order, per year, and for currency exchange. On €1,000, a €10 fee is already 1%.

### 5 · Buy the whole market

1:56 Five. Buy the whole market.
1:59 Instead of guessing which company will win, buy them all, with one global index ETF.
2:05 One fund can hold well over a thousand companies across dozens of countries, for around 0.2% a year or less. In Europe, look for UCITS in the name.

### 6 · Automate it

2:15 Six. Automate it.
2:18 Many brokers let you set up a monthly savings plan, sometimes for free. Even €50 a month.
2:25 Your first €1,000 becomes the start of a habit, not a one-off bet.
2:30 €1,000 left alone for thirty years at 7% a year grows to about €7,600. Add €100 a month, and it's about €125,000.
2:42 About €37,000 of that is money you put in. And that's before fees, inflation and tax.

### 7 · Then don't touch it

2:50 Seven. Then don't touch it.
2:53 In early 2020, stock markets fell about a third in a few weeks. People who sold locked in the loss.
2:59 The S&P 500, the big US index, was back at its record about five months after the bottom.
3:06 It isn't always that fast. After 2000, US stocks needed about seven years to get back, then 2008 hit. Thirteen years in all. You can lose money.
3:19 Don't check it every day. Once a month is plenty. Once a year is better.

### 8 · Keep learning, slowly

3:25 Eight. Keep learning, slowly.
3:28 Read one good book. Watch the fees. Ignore anyone promising fast, guaranteed returns.
3:35 If it sounds too good to be true, it usually is.

### Final message

3:39 Final message.
3:41 The best first investment isn't a stock. It's a system: no expensive debt, a cushion, the right account, one cheap global fund, and automatic deposits.
3:51 Boring? Yes. That's exactly why it works.
