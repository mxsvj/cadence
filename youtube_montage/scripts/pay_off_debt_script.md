# How to Pay Off Debt So Fast It Feels Illegal

Durée : 3:33 · style doodle · voix Kokoro bf_emma

Sources (vérifiées en octobre 2026) :

- Gal & McShane (2012), Can Fighting Small Battles Help Win the War? Evidence from Consumer Debt Management (Journal of Marketing Research) : https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/to_beat_debt_consider_starting_small
- Kettle, Trudel, Blanchard & Häubl (2016), Repayment Concentration and Consumer Motivation to Get Out of Debt (Journal of Consumer Research) : https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2017/01/the-secret-to-paying-off-credit-card-debt.html

Example (hypothetical): €3,000 at 20% a year, interest calculated monthly. €60 a month: about 9 years and €3,500 of interest. €150 a month: about 2 years and €680 of interest. If debt feels unmanageable, look for a free debt advice service in your country.

## Texte dit (= sous-titres)

0:00 You pay every month. And somehow, the debt barely moves.
0:04 That's not because you're bad with money. Some debt is built to be slow.
0:08 Interest is added every month. If your payment barely covers it, you're running on the spot.
0:14 And here's the part nobody tells you.
0:18 The minimum payment is the most expensive button in your banking app.
0:22 Here are six steps to get out faster. Let's begin.

### 1 · Look at the whole monster

0:26 One. Look at the whole monster.
0:29 Write down every debt: how much you owe, the interest rate, and the minimum payment.
0:34 Include everything: cards, store credit, buy now pay later, car loans, even money you owe friends.
0:41 Most people have never seen the total in one place. It's scary for ten minutes. Then it's a plan.

### 2 · Escape the minimum payment

0:47 Two. Escape the minimum payment.
0:50 Say you owe €3,000 on a card at 20% a year.
0:54 Pay €60 a month, and it takes about nine years. You pay around €3,500 in interest. More than the debt itself.
1:02 Minimums are often set as a small slice of what you owe. As the debt shrinks, the payment shrinks too, so the finish line keeps moving away.
1:11 Pay €150 a month instead, and you're free in about two years, with around €680 of interest.
1:19 Same debt. Same rate. Seven years of your life back.

### 3 · Pick an order

1:23 Three. Pick an order.
1:25 The avalanche: pay the minimum on everything, and throw every extra euro at the highest interest rate first. Mathematically, it saves the most.
1:34 The snowball: smallest balance first. You close accounts quickly, and each win keeps you going.
1:40 In one study of about six thousand people in a debt programme, those who cleared small accounts first were more likely to clear all of their debt.
1:48 So if the rates are similar, start small. If one rate is much higher, kill that one first.

### 4 · One debt at a time

1:54 Four. One debt at a time.
1:57 Spreading your extra money thinly across every debt feels fair. But then nothing seems to move.
2:03 Researchers found that people who focused their repayments on one account at a time felt more progress, and paid more aggressively.
2:11 So pay the minimum on all the others, and put every extra euro on one. When it's gone, roll that payment into the next.

### 5 · Make the interest smaller

2:19 Five. Make the interest smaller.
2:21 Call your bank and ask for a lower rate. It costs nothing to ask.
2:25 A cheaper consolidation loan can help too, but only if you stop using the old cards. Check every fee first.
2:33 Even a few points matter. On €3,000, going from 20% to 10% cuts the interest from about €600 a year to about €300.

### 6 · Don't refill the bucket

2:44 Six. Don't refill the bucket.
2:47 Keep a small emergency fund, even €500, so the next surprise doesn't go straight back on the card.
2:54 And when a debt is gone, send its old payment to the next one. Then, to your savings.
2:59 And keep it visible: a list on the fridge, crossed out one line at a time.

### Final message

3:05 Final message.
3:07 Debt isn't a character flaw. It's a maths problem, and maths problems can be solved.
3:12 List it. Pay more than the minimum. Pick an order. One at a time. Lower the rate. Don't refill it.
