# Compound Interest Explained So Simply It Feels Illegal

Durée : 4:56 · style doodle · voix Kokoro bf_emma

Sources (vérifiées en octobre 2026) :

- UBS Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2025 (Dimson, Marsh, Staunton): world equities ≈5.2% a year above inflation, 1900–2024 : https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/?p=280729
- European Central Bank: 2% inflation target : https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/strategy/html/index.en.html

All examples are hypothetical: 7% a year before fees and inflation; the €10,000 example compounds yearly, the monthly-deposit examples compound monthly (7%/12 a month). Rule of 72: €10,000 becomes about €20,000, €39,000 and €76,000 after 10, 20 and 30 years. €200 a month from 25 to 65: about €525,000 (€96,000 put in); from 35 to 65: about €244,000 (€72,000 put in); €400 a month from 35 to 65: about €488,000 (€144,000 put in), still less than Anna. At 2% inflation, cash loses about 45% of its buying power in 30 years. Fees: 0.2% vs 1.8% a year on the same deposits for 30 years, about €235,000 vs €173,000, about €62,000 less. The stock chart in chapter 7 is an illustration, not real data. Past returns are not a promise of future returns. All figures are before tax.

0:00 Leave €10,000 alone for 30 years, and it turns into about €76,000.
0:06 At 7% a year, you don't add a single euro. The money does the work.
0:11 That's compound interest. Slow, then boring, then suddenly unbelievable. Most people quit during the boring part.

### 1 · Interest on interest

0:25 One. Interest on interest.
0:28 Say you have €100, and it grows 10% a year.
0:32 After one year, you have €110.
0:36 Next year, you don't earn 10% on €100. You earn it on €110.
0:42 Your interest starts earning its own interest.
0:45 It's a snowball. Small at the top of the hill. Huge at the bottom.
0:50 Same snow, same slope. The only thing that changed is time.

### 2 · The rule of 72

0:55 Two. The rule of 72.
0:58 Want to know how fast your money doubles? Divide 72 by the yearly return.
1:04 At 7% a year, 72 divided by 7 is about 10. Your money doubles roughly every ten years.
1:12 So €10,000 becomes about €20,000, then €39,000, then €76,000, the number from the start.
1:20 Each doubling is bigger than all the ones before it.

### 3 · Time beats money

1:24 Three. Time beats money.
1:27 Meet Anna and Ben. Both invest €200 a month, at 7% a year.
1:33 Anna starts at 25. Ben starts at 35. Both stop at 65.
1:40 Ben puts in €72,000. He ends with about €244,000.
1:46 Anna puts in only €24,000 more. She ends with about €525,000.
1:53 Even if Ben doubles it to €400 a month, he ends with about €488,000. Still less than Anna.
2:02 Anna ends with more than twice as much as Ben. Starting early beats starting big.

### 4 · The boring years

2:08 Four. The boring years.
2:11 Here's why most people give up. After ten years of €200 a month, Anna has about €35,000.
2:19 Only about €11,000 of that is growth. It feels like nothing is happening.
2:25 But around year 11, something flips. One year of growth beats everything she puts in that year.
2:30 After that, the curve bends upward. The boring part was the foundation.

### 5 · It works against you too

2:36 Five. It works against you too.
2:39 Compound interest doesn't care which side you're on.
2:42 Store credit and credit cards can charge 20% a year.
2:46 At 20%, a debt doubles in less than four years. That's the rule of 72, working for the bank.
2:53 So before you invest, pay off expensive debt. It's the best return you'll ever get.

### 6 · Fees compound too

2:59 Six. Fees compound too.
3:02 A fee of 1.8% a year sounds tiny. It isn't.
3:07 €200 a month for 30 years: with fees of 0.2%, about €235,000. With 1.8%, about €173,000.
3:20 Same money in. About €62,000 less out. The fee compounds against you, every single year.

### 7 · Inflation: compounding in reverse

3:28 Seven. Inflation is compounding in reverse.
3:33 Prices rise a little every year. The European Central Bank aims for 2%.
3:38 At 2% inflation, cash loses about 45% of its buying power in 30 years.
3:45 The same goes for every number in this video. In today's money, that €76,000 from the start is worth about €42,000. Still four times what went in.
3:56 That's why long-term money can't just sit still. Over the last 125 years, global stocks grew about 5% a year faster than inflation, on average.
4:06 Not every year. Some years were terrible. But over decades, time did the heavy lifting.

4:28 And then, the hardest part: do nothing, for a very long time.
