# Compound Interest Explained So Simply It Feels Illegal

Durée : 4:25 · style doodle · voix Kokoro bf_emma

Sources (vérifiées en octobre 2026) :

- UBS Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2025 (Dimson, Marsh, Staunton): world equities ≈5.2% a year above inflation, 1900–2024 : https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/?p=280729
- European Central Bank: 2% inflation target : https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/strategy/html/index.en.html
- Quote Investigator: the "eighth wonder of the world" quote and Einstein : https://quoteinvestigator.com/2011/10/18/compound-interest/

All examples are hypothetical: 7% a year before fees, monthly deposits compounded monthly. €200 a month from 25 to 65: about €525,000 (€96,000 put in); from 35 to 65: about €244,000 (€72,000 put in). Fees: 0.2% vs 1.8% a year on the same deposits for 30 years, about €235,000 vs €173,000. Past returns are not a promise of future returns.

## Texte dit (= sous-titres)

0:00 Imagine putting €10,000 away, and never touching it again.
0:04 Thirty years later, at 7% a year, it's worth about €76,000.
0:10 You added nothing. Not one euro. The money did the work.
0:14 This is compound interest. It's often called the eighth wonder of the world.
0:18 That quote is usually credited to Einstein. He almost certainly never said it. But the maths is real.
0:25 And here's the part nobody tells you.
0:28 Compound interest is slow, then boring, then suddenly unbelievable. Most people quit during the boring part.
0:36 Today, I'll explain it so simply it feels illegal. Seven ideas. No formulas. Let's begin.

### 1 · Interest on interest

0:42 One. Interest on interest.
0:45 Say you have €100, and it grows 10% a year.
0:49 After one year, you have €110.
0:52 Next year, you don't earn 10% on €100. You earn it on €110.
0:58 Your interest starts earning its own interest.
1:01 It's a snowball. Small at the top of the hill. Huge at the bottom.
1:05 Same snow, same slope. The only thing that changed is time.

### 2 · The rule of 72

1:09 Two. The rule of 72.
1:12 Want to know how fast your money doubles? Divide 72 by the yearly return.
1:17 At 7% a year, 72 divided by 7 is about 10. Your money doubles roughly every ten years.
1:25 So €10,000 becomes €20,000, then €40,000, then €80,000.
1:31 Each doubling is bigger than all the ones before it.

### 3 · Time beats money

1:35 Three. Time beats money.
1:37 Meet Anna and Ben. Both invest €200 a month, at 7% a year.
1:42 Anna starts at 25. Ben starts at 35. Both stop at 65.
1:48 Ben puts in €72,000. He ends with about €244,000.
1:54 Anna puts in only €24,000 more. She ends with about €525,000.
2:01 Ten extra years more than doubled her money. Starting early beats starting big.

### 4 · The boring years

2:07 Four. The boring years.
2:09 Here's why most people give up. After ten years of €200 a month, Anna has about €35,000.
2:17 Only about €11,000 of that is growth. It feels like nothing is happening.
2:22 But around year 11, the growth in a single year becomes bigger than everything she puts in that year.
2:28 After that, the curve bends upward. The boring part was the foundation.

### 5 · It works against you too

2:33 Five. It works against you too.
2:36 Compound interest doesn't care which side you're on.
2:39 Some credit cards and store credit charge around 20% a year.
2:43 At 20%, a debt doubles in less than four years. That's the rule of 72, working for the bank.
2:50 So before you invest, pay off expensive debt. It's the best return you'll ever get.

### 6 · Fees compound too

2:56 Six. Fees compound too.
2:58 A fee of 1.8% a year sounds tiny. It isn't.
3:03 €200 a month for 30 years: with fees of 0.2%, about €235,000. With 1.8%, about €173,000.
3:16 Same money in. Over €60,000 less out. The fee compounds against you, every single year.

### 7 · Inflation: compounding in reverse

3:23 Seven. Inflation is compounding in reverse.
3:26 Prices rise a little every year. The European Central Bank aims for 2%.
3:31 At 2% inflation, money sitting in cash loses almost half its buying power in 30 years.
3:38 That's why long-term money usually can't just sit still. Over the last 125 years, global stocks grew about 5% a year faster than inflation, on average.
3:49 Not every year. Some years were terrible. But over decades, time did the heavy lifting.

### Final message

3:55 Final message.
3:57 Compound interest isn't magic. It's just patience, written in maths.
4:02 Start early. Start small if you have to. Keep fees low. Kill expensive debt.
4:07 And then, the hardest part: do nothing, for a very long time.
