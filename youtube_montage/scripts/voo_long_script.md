## Intro

- If you watch American finance YouTube, you've heard it a thousand times: just buy VOO.
- So you open your broker in Europe, you type VOO, and... nothing. Not available in your region.
- There's a reason for that. And there's a simple way around it.
- In this video: what VOO actually is, why Europe blocks it, and the European version you can buy instead, for almost the same price.

## Part 1 — What is VOO?

- VOO is an ETF. Think of it as a basket. With one click, you buy a tiny piece of many companies at once.
- This basket follows the S&P 500: about 500 big American companies. Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, and hundreds more.
- VOO is run by Vanguard, and it's famous for one reason: it's incredibly cheap. 0.03% a year. On $10,000, that's $3.
- It launched in 2010. Today, it's one of the world's biggest funds.
- And it's an index fund: nobody picks the winners. You simply own the whole market, the good and the bad.
- That's why every American video says: buy it. But those videos are made for Americans.

## Part 2 — Why Europe blocks it

- In 2018, the European Union started applying a rule called PRIIPs. It's there to protect everyday investors, people like you and me.
- The rule says: if a product is sold to regular investors, it must come with a short document. It's called a KID. Key Information Document.
- Three pages, maximum. What the product is. How risky it is, on a scale from one to seven. What it costs. And what could happen to your money.
- The idea is simple: every product explained the same way, so you can compare them.
- And it applies everywhere in the European Union, from France to Germany to Spain.
- Most American funds simply don't make this document. Europe is a small market for them, and the template clashes with US rules.
- No KID, no sale. So your broker isn't being difficult. It's following the law.
- And it's not just one broker. Trade Republic, Degiro, Interactive Brokers: for regular clients, they all block it.

## Part 3 — Is there a loophole?

- Technically, yes. If you're classified as a professional client, you can buy US funds.
- But you need two out of three: a portfolio above €500,000, about ten large trades per quarter, for a whole year, or a year working in finance.
- And you give up some of your protections. For most of us, that's not the way. There's a much simpler one.

## Part 4 — The European twin

- Europe has its own fund rules. They're called UCITS, and they've existed since 1985.
- Many fund companies, including Vanguard itself, make S&P 500 ETFs under those rules. Same index, same companies, with a KID. So you're allowed to buy them.
- Don't be confused by the names. One fund can have several tickers, depending on the stock exchange. CSPX in London, SXR8 in Germany: same fund, different name tag.
- Most of them are based in Ireland. And that's not random.
- Ireland and Luxembourg are Europe's two big fund hubs. But for American stocks, Ireland has an edge.
- When an American company pays a dividend to a fund outside the US, the US keeps part of it as tax. Usually 30%.
- But Ireland signed a tax treaty with the US. For an Irish fund, it's only 15%. That's money that stays invested for you.
- And there's a bonus. If you're not American and you die holding US funds, the US can tax your estate above $60,000, depending on your country. Irish funds generally avoid that.
- So, the twin of VOO is an S&P 500 UCITS ETF, usually domiciled in Ireland. Domiciled just means where the fund is legally based.

## Part 5 — What does it cost you?

- The big ones, from iShares and Vanguard, charge 0.07% a year. Some are around 0.05%.
- Compared to VOO, that's a difference of about €4 a year on €10,000. About the price of a coffee.
- And you never get a bill. The fee comes out of the fund, a tiny bit every day. It's already in the price.
- For comparison, many funds sold by banks charge more than 1% a year. That's where the real money goes.
- Now, three things beginners often get wrong.
- One. Buying it in euros doesn't remove the dollar. The companies are American, so the value still moves with the dollar. Only funds marked hedged try to cancel that.
- Two. The price of one share doesn't matter. A share at €500? Not more expensive than one at €50. What matters is the fee, and what's inside.
- Three. You'll see two versions: accumulating and distributing. Accumulating reinvests the dividends for you. Distributing pays them into your account.
- Which one is better depends on where you live and how you're taxed. That's for the next video.
- So, here's the checklist. Search for an S&P 500 UCITS ETF. Check the yearly fee. Check that it's based in Ireland. And pick accumulating or distributing.

- Over the long run, it has gone up. But the past guarantees nothing. Only invest money you won't need for years.
