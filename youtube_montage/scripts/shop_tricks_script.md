# 7 Tricks Shops Use to Make You Spend More (and How to Beat Them)

## Texte dit (= sous-titres)

0:00 You go online to buy one thing. You leave with four.
0:03 And somehow, it felt like you saved money.
0:06 That's not bad luck. It's design.
0:09 Shops test prices, buttons, colours and timers on millions of people. Your brain is the thing they're testing.
0:16 And here's the part nobody tells you.
0:19 Once you can see the tricks, most of them stop working.
0:22 Here are seven of them, and how to beat each one. Let's begin.

### 1 · The price that ends in 9

0:27 One. The price that ends in nine.
0:30 €9.99 feels much closer to nine than to ten. Your brain reads the first digit, and mostly stops there.
0:37 In one experiment, a clothing catalogue raised the price of a dress from $34 to $39. It sold about a third more.
0:45 At $44, it sold the same as at $34.
0:50 The fix: round prices up in your head. €9.99 is ten euros. €49.95 is fifty.

### 2 · The decoy

0:58 Two. The decoy.
1:00 A magazine once offered three options. Online only: $59. Print only: $125. Print and online: $125.
1:11 Nobody picks print only. It's there to make the bundle look like a steal.
1:15 In a famous class experiment, 84% of students chose the bundle.
1:20 When the decoy was removed, 68% chose the cheap online option instead.
1:26 The fix: when you see three prices, ask which one is only there to push you. Then compare the other two.

### 3 · The fake countdown

1:32 Three. The fake countdown.
1:35 Only two left. Offer ends in nine minutes. Someone in Paris just bought this.
1:40 Urgency switches off the slow, careful part of your brain.
1:43 In a check published in 2023, EU consumer authorities looked at 399 online shops. 148 used at least one manipulative trick.
1:54 42 of them had countdown timers that were fake.
1:58 The fix: if a timer says hurry, wait. A real deal survives a night of sleep.

### 4 · The fake discount

2:03 Four. The fake discount.
2:06 Was €199. Now €99. Half price! But was it ever really €199?
2:14 A high before price is an anchor. It makes the new price feel small.
2:19 In the EU, since 2022, a shop announcing a discount has to compare it with its lowest price in the previous thirty days.
2:26 So check that small line, or a price tracker, before you believe the percentage.

### 5 · Free shipping

2:32 Five. Free shipping.
2:34 Shipping costs €4.90. It's free above €50. Your basket is at €38.
2:40 So you add a €15 thing you didn't plan to buy, to save €4.90.
2:46 You just spent €10 more to avoid paying €4.90.
2:49 The fix: pay the shipping, or wait until you really need more.

### 6 · Buy now, pay later

2:54 Six. Buy now, pay later.
2:57 Four payments of €25 feel lighter than one payment of €100. It's the same €100.
3:04 One large study of US bank data found that once people start using buy now, pay later, their total spending goes up, and stays up for months.
3:13 New EU consumer credit rules, due to apply from November 2026, bring most buy now, pay later under credit rules, with checks on whether you can afford it.
3:24 The fix: if you can't pay for it today, you probably can't afford it today.

### 7 · The store map

3:29 Seven. The store map.
3:31 Milk and bread are usually at the back. To reach them, you walk past everything else.
3:36 And the sweets wait at the checkout, right where you stand still with nothing to do.
3:41 England even banned less healthy snacks from the checkouts of larger stores in 2022.
3:46 The fix: shop with a list, and walk straight to what's on it.

### Final message

3:50 Final message.
3:53 Some of these tricks are banned. Most are perfectly legal. They're just very good design.
3:58 Round prices up. Spot the decoy. Ignore the timer. Check the real old price. Pay the shipping. Pay in full. Bring a list.
4:06 Your brain isn't stupid. It's just being tested by people who do this all day.
