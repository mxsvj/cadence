DESCRIPTION (à coller telle quelle)
Leave €10,000 alone for 30 years at 7% a year and it turns into about €76,000 (about €42,000 in today's money, after 2% inflation: still four times what went in). That's compound interest. Here are 7 simple ideas: interest on interest, the rule of 72, why time beats money, why most people quit during the boring years, and how debt, fees and inflation compound against you.

Education only, not financial advice.

All examples are hypothetical: 7% a year before fees and inflation; the €10,000 example compounds yearly, the monthly-deposit examples compound monthly (7%/12 a month). Rule of 72: €10,000 becomes about €20,000, €39,000 and €76,000 after 10, 20 and 30 years. €200 a month from 25 to 65: about €525,000 (€96,000 put in); from 35 to 65: about €244,000 (€72,000 put in); €400 a month from 35 to 65: about €488,000 (€144,000 put in), still less than Anna. At 2% inflation, cash loses about 45% of its buying power in 30 years. Fees: 0.2% vs 1.8% a year on the same deposits for 30 years, about €235,000 vs €173,000, about €62,000 less. The stock chart in chapter 7 is an illustration, not real data. Past returns are not a promise of future returns. All figures are before tax.
