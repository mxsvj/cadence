DESCRIPTION (à coller telle quelle)
You're 25, you live in Europe, and every month you quietly put €200 into an S&P 500 ETF. Thirty years later, the account says almost a quarter of a million euros. But this isn't a story about getting rich. It's about what happens in between: the first crash, the friends who seem to do better, the debt, the layoff, the fee nobody sees, and the boring months nobody talks about.

A story, not a promise. Every number is a hypothesis. Education only, not financial advice.

THE HYPOTHESES
€200 a month for 30 years, 7% a year after the ETF's fee, compounded monthly: about €244,000 for €72,000 put in.
Bank fund at 1.8% a year instead of 0.07%: about €175,000 (≈ €69,000 less).
Started at 35 instead of 25: about €104,000 at 55.
4% a year taken out of €244,000: about €9,800 a year. €244,000 in 30 years at 2% inflation: about €135,000 in today's money.
Market facts used in the story: the S&P 500 fell about 34% in February–March 2020 and was back at its old high about five months after the bottom; it fell about 20% in 2022 and set a new high about two years later.
Real returns go up and down, and nothing is guaranteed. Charts marked "illustration" are illustrations, not real price data. Taxes depend on your country; check your local rules.
