# POV: You're 25 and Invest €200 a Month — voice-over script (2026 remake)

Refonte de `pov_25_europe_15min_script.md` : voix Emma (Kokoro), mouvement, une couleur par décennie, et les lignes les plus lentes coupées (15 min → 13 min 31).
Hypothèse partout : 200 €/mois, 7 % par an après les frais de l'ETF, capitalisation mensuelle. Chaque ligne = un plan.

## Intro

- POV: you're 25, you live in Europe, and every month, you quietly put €200 into an S&P 500 ETF.
- Thirty years later, the account says almost a quarter of a million euros.
- But this isn't a story about getting rich. It's a story about what happens in between.
- The doubts. The crashes. The friends who seem to do better. And the boring months nobody talks about.
- Every number in this story is a hypothesis: 7% a year, after fees. Real life will be different. This is not financial advice.
- Let's go back to the beginning.

## Âge 25 — The first paycheck

- Age 25. The first paycheck.
- You just got your first real salary. After rent, food and transport, there's a little left at the end of the month.
- Your friends know exactly what to do with it. New phone. Weekend trips. A car on credit.
- You don't judge them. You just want something different: to stop worrying about money one day.
- Your bank says: leave it in the savings account. It pays almost nothing, but at least it's safe.
- But prices keep rising. So money that sits still slowly loses value, even if the number never moves.
- So one evening, you make a decision. Not a big one. Just €200 a month.
- Before that, you put three months of expenses aside. An emergency fund. Because life happens.

## Âge 25 — The first buy

- The first buy.
- You open your broker app, you type VOO, and... nothing. Not available in your region.
- That's the fund every American video tells you to buy. But Europe has its own rules, and VOO doesn't follow them.
- So you search for the European version: an S&P 500 UCITS ETF, based in Ireland. Same 500 companies.
- The yearly fee is 0.07%. On your first €200, that's 14 cents a year.
- The app shows €200.00. The next day, €199.40. Welcome to investing.
- For comparison, many bank funds charge more than 1%. Remember that number, it'll come back.
- You set up an automatic plan: every month, on payday, €200 goes in. You don't have to think about it.
- You now own a tiny piece of Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, and almost 500 other companies. It feels like nothing.

## Âge 26 — The boring years

- Age 26. The boring years.
- The first months, you check the app every day. Up a little. Down a little. Up again.
- One day the account is down €30. You lose sleep over thirty euros. It's almost funny, looking back.
- After a while, you stop checking. The plan runs on its own.
- Your friend Max tells you about a coin that tripled in a week. He made more in seven days than you in a year.
- You feel stupid. Slow. Old-fashioned.
- Three months later, Max's coin is down 80%. He doesn't talk about it anymore.
- You didn't win big. But you didn't lose big either. That's the deal you signed.
- Some months you forget the plan even exists. That's when it works best.

## Âge 28 — Love and debt

- Age 28. Love and debt.
- At 28, you move in with someone you love. One evening, they show you a number they've been hiding: €4,000 of credit card debt.
- The interest is around 20% a year. That's money leaking out every single month.
- Here's a simple rule: no investment reliably beats paying off debt like that. Paying it off is like earning 20%, guaranteed.
- So you keep your €200 going, and together you throw everything else at the debt.
- Eight months later, it's gone. You celebrate with pizza on the floor of a half-empty apartment.

## Âge 30 — The first crash

- Age 30. The first crash.
- Five years in, you've put in €12,000. Your account shows about €14,300.
- Then the news changes. A virus. Cities closing. Markets falling every day.
- It's like 2020: in just a few weeks, the S&P 500 lost about a third of its value.
- Your €14,300 becomes €9,450. On paper, you just lost almost five thousand euros.
- Your finger is on the sell button.
- But you remember why you started. You didn't need this money for years. So you do nothing.
- And your €200 keeps buying every month, at lower prices. Same money, more shares.
- In 2020, about five months after the bottom, the market was back at its old high.
- Nobody can promise it'll always be that fast. Sometimes it takes years. But the people who sold locked in their loss.

## Âge 32 — The raise

- Age 32. The raise.
- You get a raise. Your colleague Tom gets one too. He buys a new car. You buy... nothing new.
- Tom's car loses value the day he drives it away. Your €200 doesn't come with a monthly payment.
- Not because you're cheap. Because you already like your life. And extra money gives you something better: options.
- This is the most underrated move in personal finance: when you earn more, don't automatically spend more.

## Âge 34 — The house question

- Age 34. The house question.
- At 34, everyone around you is buying an apartment. You wonder if you should stop investing to save for a deposit.
- Here's how you think about it. Money you need in two or three years shouldn't be in the stock market.
- Because the market can drop by a third right when you need it.
- So you split your money into two buckets. The €200 keeps going for the long run, and a separate savings account grows for the house.
- Different goals, different places. That one idea saves people from a lot of panic.

## Âge 35 — The quiet milestone

- Age 35. The quiet milestone.
- Ten years. 120 squares.
- You've put in €24,000. Your account shows almost €35,000.
- And something quiet happens: this year, your money earns about as much as you put in yourself.
- From here, you're no longer doing all the work alone.
- Nobody throws you a party. There's no notification. Just a number on a screen, growing quietly.

## Âge 37 — The fee nobody sees

- Age 37. The fee nobody sees.
- At a family dinner, your cousin says she invests too. Her bank picked a fund for her.
- You ask about the fee. She doesn't know. You look it up together: 1.8% a year.
- It sounds small. 1.8 versus 0.07. But over thirty years, on €200 a month, here's the gap.
- You end up with about €244,000. She ends up with about €175,000, in the same market.
- Almost seventy thousand euros, gone in fees she never saw on a bill.
- She switches the next week. It's never too late, but every year counts.

## Âge 40 — Taxes and choices

- Age 40. Taxes and choices.
- At 40, you finally look at the boring part: taxes. Every European country handles this differently.
- Some countries have special accounts that cut the tax, if you follow their rules. In France, for example, there's the PEA.
- Your fund is accumulating: the dividends are reinvested automatically. In some countries, that can save tax. In others, it changes nothing.
- So the right account can matter as much as the right fund. Check your tax office's website, or ask a local adviser.
- You also check your fund once a year: the fee, the size, and that it still does what it says. Ten minutes. That's all the maintenance.

## Âge 42 — The guaranteed offer

- Age 42. The guaranteed offer.
- At 42, an ad follows you everywhere. A man in a rented sports car, promising 20% a month. Guaranteed.
- You do the math. 20% a month would turn €1,000 into almost €9,000 in a year. If that were real, he wouldn't need your money.
- The rule is simple. High returns and a guarantee never come together. When you see both, it's a trap.
- Your boring ETF has never promised you anything. That's exactly why you trust it.

## Âge 44 — The layoff

- Age 44. The layoff.
- At 44, your company announces cuts. Your name is on the list.
- It's scary. But this is exactly what the emergency fund was for. Three months of expenses, waiting since you were 25.
- You don't sell anything. The emergency fund pays the bills, and the €200 keeps going.
- Four months later, you find a new job. Your investments never even noticed.
- That's the real job of an emergency fund. It protects your investments from your life.

## Âge 45 — The snowball

- Age 45. The snowball.
- Twenty years. 240 squares. You've put in €48,000.
- Your account shows about €104,000. More than double what you put in.
- This year, your money earns about €7,300. You only add €2,400. It earns three times more than you.
- That's compounding. Your gains make their own gains. At first it's invisible. Then it's all you see.
- If you had started at 35 instead of 25, with the same €200, you'd have about €104,000 at 55, not €244,000.
- That's why starting early matters so much. The first euros you invest work the longest.

## Âge 47 — The record high

- Age 47. The record high.
- At 47, the market hits a new record. On TV, experts say it's too expensive. Wait for the crash, they say.
- The problem is that nobody knows when the crash comes. Waiting often costs more than the crash itself.
- Records are normal. A market that grows over time keeps setting new ones.
- So you don't wait. You don't guess. The €200 goes in, like every month for twenty-two years.

## Âge 50 — The second drop

- Age 50. The second drop.
- At 50, your account is around €162,000. Then the market falls again, like in 2022: about 20% in a year.
- This time, 20% isn't a few thousand euros. It's about €32,000. More than you saved in ten years.
- Your hands are calm. You've seen this before. You know the only real loss is the one you lock in by selling.
- About two years later, the market is back above its old high.
- But it makes you think about something: the S&P 500 is only American companies.
- Some Europeans prefer a world ETF, with thousands of companies from many countries. It's a valid choice, explained in our ETF video.

## Âge 55 — The number

- Age 55. The number.
- Thirty years. 360 squares. Every single one filled.
- You've put in €72,000. The account says about €244,000.
- Is that rich? Not really. But it's freedom.
- A common rule of thumb says you could take out about 4% a year. That's around €9,800 a year, or €800 a month.
- Not a fortune. But €800 a month, every month, without working for it. That changes how you say yes and no.
- And remember inflation: in today's money, €244,000 in thirty years is worth about €135,000.
- So no, you're not a millionaire. You're something quieter: someone who doesn't panic when the car breaks down.
- And the strangest part? The hardest thing you did wasn't picking a fund. It was staying still.
- If you remember one thing: time, a cheap fund, and not stopping. That's the whole trick.
- And if you're 35, 45 or 50 right now, it's not too late. The squares just start from where you are.
- All numbers here assume 7% a year after fees. Markets can go down for years, and nothing is guaranteed. This is not financial advice.
- Maybe you're 25 right now. Maybe this is your first month. Square number one.
